Which industries and regions drive the change—and does more value mean more output?

Follow five financial years of production, then look more closely at the places and markets behind the national figures. Dollar values measure the value of production at the farm gate, before wholesale and retail costs. They measure neither profit nor inflation-adjusted growth.

01

THE NATIONAL PICTURE

Fruit and vegetable values rise;
nursery remains below 2020/21.

Between 2020/21 and 2024/25, fruit production value grew by 25.4% and vegetable value by 23.0%. Nursery value ended 1.2% below its starting point. Compare physical output to see how differently these industries developed.

Sources: Hort Innovation, 2024/25 handbook, five-year sector tables; ABS, 2024/25 horticulture data cube, commodity tables.

02

WHERE IT GROWS

National totals hide
regional differences.

Production follows different geographic patterns for different industries. Compare state dollar values, avocado output per bearing hectare and the concentration of nursery production. A large state does not necessarily produce more.

REGIONAL CONTRAST

In 2024/25, Queensland's avocado output rose 19.3%, while Western Australia's fell 43.9%. ABS reports industry feedback linking WA's fall to alternate bearing: trees can alternate between heavier and lighter crops.

Sources: ABS horticulture data cubes and experimental avocado estimates, 2024/25; ABS ASGS Edition 3 state boundaries, simplified for display.

03

VALUE AND PHYSICAL OUTPUT

More dollars can accompany
less produce.

In 2024/25, vegetable production value rose 5.6% while volume fell 1.4%. Avocados show an even sharper contrast: 22.0% more value alongside 12.9% less output. Changes in product mix and prices can affect value, so the two measures need to be read together.

Sources: ABS 2024/25 commodity data cube; Hort Innovation's nursery statistics, sourced from the NY21000 industry statistics project.

04

MARKETS AND INDUSTRY DEVELOPMENT

What is grown
does not all reach the fresh market.

Processing, fresh exports and domestic fresh supply serve different markets. Potatoes—the largest vegetable industry by production value—make this distinction visible. Alongside those market differences, the avocado case study shows bearing land expanding while grower business numbers fall.

Sources: Hort Innovation, 2024/25 fruit, vegetable and nursery tables; potato chapter, printed pages 339–340; ABS selected-crop data cube, avocado estimates.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE SHOWS

Read value, output
and place together.

Australia's fruit, vegetable and nursery industries reached a combined production value of $16.13 billion in 2024/25. That total contains different experiences: expanding fruit output, declining vegetable volume, a partial nursery recovery, and sharp regional contrasts. The markets supplied and the structure of growers add context to the headline growth.

Sources & definitions

All financial years end in June. Values are in current Australian dollars. Nursery plant units and produce tonnes are different measures. Missing or confidential figures are not converted to zero.
